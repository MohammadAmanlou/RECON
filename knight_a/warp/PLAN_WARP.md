# KNIGHT-A: when do higher-order prerequisites improve adaptive learner diagnosis?
Mohammad Amanlou — 27 November 2026 — focused research-plan draft

**Question.** Can set-valued competence dependencies reduce the number of questions needed to recover an individual learner profile relative to pairwise models, and when does structural uncertainty erase that advantage? We use KNIGHT linear algebra (20 original recognition MCQs, 11 semantic families, six binary skills), with source-traceable, curator-authored candidate dependencies. These are probabilistic mastery dependencies, not demonstrated causal learning prerequisites.

**Core approach.** Retain a Bayesian learner state and information-gain question selection as reference. Develop a structure-aware decision planner in which candidate additive, conjunctive and alternative prerequisite models produce priors over mastery. A question has a shared binary likelihood and no semantic family is repeated. The conceptual hypothesis is that higher-order interactions can be invisible to individual items but detectable through complementary question bundles; their diagnostic value depends on the item bank, uncertainty in structure and question budget, rather than on universal hypergraph expressivity.

**Method and mathematical agenda.** WARP uses unnormalized Boolean-Walsh moments of the learner belief. Multiplication by a question likelihood updates moments through symmetric differences of their masks. A backward closure indexed by remaining item families and budget preserves all terminal skill moments needed for Bayes Hamming decisions. The current exact planner uses this closure and a response-channel lower bound to prune actions safely. An outer fixed-model minimax-regret game uses exact mixture-prior best responses, with a finite-game optimality interval. The separate witness audit measures total variation between perturbations of candidate higher-order interactions; next we will investigate witness-guided action selection and rank-adaptive approximate closure with error bounds. Generic value-directed belief compression, decision-region design, risk-based CD-CAT and robust Bayesian design already exist: the research question is whether the restricted spectral representation and complementary question structure yield a nontrivial efficiency or reliability advantage beyond these methods. Neural amortization of the moment transitions and policy is a conditional scale-up, not a prerequisite for the pilot.

**Evidence.** The unchanged eight-question v1.1 pilot reports 60 held-out worlds and six policies. Conjunctive-vs-additive IG Hamming improvement is .00792 [ .00158, .01383 ] in its mismatch regime, but no clear improvement in matched/noisy conditions; no universal superiority. Its IRT is a synthetic scalar proxy and its BKT-style baseline static. The new independent exploratory two-question WARP pilot runs 36 worlds (2,304 learners) with seven methods and fixed parameters, common potential responses and paired world intervals. WARP Hamming .27973/.30621/.28103 in conjunctive/additive/shift, versus mixture MER .28038/.30339/.28668. It is worse in additive and not consistently better than strong baselines. Exact dense and spectral planning agree (.3195464); pruning reduces evaluated nodes from 1,473 to 405 on the uniform mixture. Dense pruning control confirms the node gain is due to pruning; spectral compression reduces visited representation entries but speed is unestablished. Finite candidate-model regret gap .00563; not statistical mastery certification. All four candidate interaction masks have zero single-item and positive two-item observability under the specified parity perturbation. Code, per-world data, tests and browser demos are in RECON.

**Next tests.** Separate development worlds (fit priors, likelihoods and any neural policy) from held-out worlds and genuinely unseen graph families. Compare at equal budgets with random, IRT-CAT, static and temporal BKT as appropriate, pairwise and higher-order diagnostic models, risk-based CD-CAT, factorized/value-directed planning and robust design. Report Hamming, exact recovery, questions to recovery (censored failures), calibration and runtime/memory; distinguish evidence for structural representation from the selection policy with capacity-matched ablations. Test independent compensatory, nonlinear and noisy generators and, with consent and independent assessment evidence, actual KNIGHT learners. Keep unresolved structural hypotheses explicit. A claim of statistical coverage requires separately justified confidence sets; no human benefit or universal novelty is claimed.
